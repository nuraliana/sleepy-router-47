# Candidates — One-Star Review of Something Essential

Every line below was composed from the brief alone. Avoided on purpose:
the sun, gravity and sleep (already entered by others), plus oxygen
(also entered), water and Mondays (named in the brief, likely crowded).

Scored against the judge's five criteria, in order, 1–5 each (25 max):
1. One-star review of an everyday essential
2. Sharpest true grievance
3. Holds the deadpan
4. Turns in the last few words
5. Tight and specific

## 1. Wi-Fi — 24/25 ★ WINNER

> One star. Wi-Fi: full signal in every room except the one chair I actually sit in.

82 characters. The dead-zone complaint everyone has lived: the bars are full
everywhere except the exact seat you use. The sting lands in the last few
words ("except the one chair I actually sit in"), after the complaint has
been read straight. One joke, no winking, no exclamation mark.
Scores: 5 / 4 / 5 / 5 / 5. The 4 on criterion 2: Wi-Fi dropping is a familiar
complaint in general; what sharpens it here is the chair-specific framing.

## 2. Memory — 23/25

> One star. Memory remembers every embarrassing thing I did in 2009 and forgets why I walked into this room.

106 characters. A true, universal grievance with a clean contrast. Loses a
point on the turn (the sting is spread across the second clause rather than
arriving in the last few words) and a point on tightness (two embarrassments
crowd one line: 2009 and the doorway).
Scores: 5 / 5 / 5 / 4 / 4.

## 3. Plumbing — 23/25

> One star. Plumbing works silently for years, then floods the bathroom at 3 a.m. on a public holiday.

100 characters. Specific and vivid — the 3 a.m. holiday flood is a real
grievance. Loses a point on the grievance (it bites homeowners/renters
unevenly, and only rarely) and a point on tightness (the setup is a little long).
Scores: 5 / 4 / 5 / 5 / 4.

## 4. Eyesight — 23/25

> One star. Eyesight was perfect for decades, then the restaurant menu print started shrinking every year.

104 characters. Sharp and specific — the shrinking menu print is a grievance
everyone over a certain age recognises instantly. Loses a point on the turn
(the joke is told by the middle of the line) and a point because the
grievance is age-gated rather than universal.
Scores: 5 / 5 / 5 / 4 / 4.

## 5. Knees — 23/25

> One star. Knees worked perfectly for twenty years, then started aching whenever the weather changes.

100 characters. True and specific — knees as weather forecasters is a
complaint filed in earnest everywhere. Loses a point on the grievance (mild
compared with the best) and a point on the turn (the sting is the whole
second clause, not the last few words).
Scores: 5 / 4 / 5 / 4 / 5.

## 6. Time — 22/25

> One star. Time drags through every meeting, flies through every holiday, and has never offered a refund.

104 characters. The refund turn is good and the deadpan holds. Loses points
because "time flies when you're having fun, drags when you're bored" is a
well-worn observation rather than a grievance never seen written down, and
the line carries two observations before the turn.
Scores: 5 / 3 / 5 / 5 / 4.

## 7. Teeth — 21/25

> One star. Teeth are the only bones you have to brush, and they still charge thousands to fix.

93 characters. A real grievance (maintenance plus expensive repair). Loses a
point on the deadpan ("the only bones you have to brush" is a quip, slightly
winking), and a point each on the turn and tightness.
Scores: 5 / 4 / 4 / 4 / 4.

## 8. Electricity — 21/25

> One star. Electricity powers the whole house until the exact second I need to save my work.

91 characters. True, but the grievance is generic — any outage line reads
the same — and the turn is soft.
Scores: 5 / 3 / 5 / 4 / 4.

## Winner

Candidate 1, Wi-Fi, 24/25 — the only candidate at champion level (24–25).
It is the freshest essential with the sharpest specific grievance, holds the
deadpan throughout, turns in its last few words, and tells one joke in
82 characters.
